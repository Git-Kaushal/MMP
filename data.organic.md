# Practical Organic Chemistry

## I. Solubility of Organic Compounds

### A. Purpose

- Preliminary identification of an unknown organic compound
- Helps classify compounds as
  - Acidic
  - Basic
  - Neutral
- Solubility depends on
  - Polarity
  - Hydrogen bonding
  - Molecular size
  - Functional groups
  - Acid-base behaviour

### B. Solubility in Water

- Water-soluble
  - Small alcohols
  - Small aldehydes
  - Small ketones
  - Small carboxylic acids
  - Amines
  - Polyhydroxy compounds
  - Amino acids
  - Small carbohydrates
- Water-insoluble
  - Hydrocarbons
  - Many aromatic compounds
  - Higher alcohols
  - Higher aldehydes and ketones
  - Many esters

### C. Solubility in Dilute HCl

- Soluble compound
  - Possible amine
- Formation of ionic salt
  - R-NH₂ + HCl → R-NH₃⁺Cl⁻
- Increased ionic character
  - Increased water solubility

### D. Solubility in Dilute NaOH

- Soluble compound
  - Possible acidic compound
- Carboxylic acid
  - R-COOH + NaOH → R-COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O
- Phenol
  - Ar-OH + NaOH → Ar-O⁻Na⁺ + H₂O

### E. Solubility in NaHCO₃

- Effervescence
  - CO₂ evolved
- Strong indication
  - Carboxylic acid
- Reaction
  - R-COOH + NaHCO₃
  - → R-COO⁻Na⁺ + CO₂↑ + H₂O

### F. Amino Acids and Solubility

- Amino acids contain
  - -NH₂
  - -COOH
- Exist predominantly as zwitterions
  - ⁺NH₃-CHR-COO⁻
- Amphoteric nature
  - React with acids
  - React with bases
- Solubility depends on
  - Side chain
  - pH
  - Isoelectric point

# II. Tests for Unsaturation

## A. Bromine Test

- Reagent
  - Bromine solution
- Positive observation
  - Decolourisation
- Suggests
  - C=C
  - C≡C

## B. Baeyer Test

- Reagent
  - Dilute alkaline KMnO₄
- Observation
  - Purple colour disappears
  - Brown MnO₂ may form
- Suggests
  - Unsaturation
  - Easily oxidised groups

---

# III. Tests for Alcohols

## A. Sodium Metal Test

- Reagent
  - Sodium metal
- Observation
  - Effervescence
- Gas
  - H₂
- Reaction
  - 2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H₂↑
- Limitation
  - Water and other acidic compounds can also react

## B. Lucas Test

- Reagents
  - Concentrated HCl
  - Anhydrous ZnCl₂
- Observation
  - Turbidity
- Relative rate
  - 3° alcohol
    - Rapid
  - 2° alcohol
    - Slower
  - 1° alcohol
    - Usually slow/no turbidity at room temperature

## C. Oxidation Test

- Primary alcohol
  - Aldehyde
  - → Carboxylic acid
- Secondary alcohol
  - Ketone
- Tertiary alcohol
  - Resistant to mild oxidation

# IV. Tests for Phenols

## A. Neutral Ferric Chloride Test

- Reagent
  - Neutral FeCl₃
- Observation
  - Characteristic coloured complex
- Suggests
  - Phenolic group

## B. Bromine Water Test

- Phenol + bromine water
- Observation
  - Decolourisation
  - White precipitate
- Product
  - 2,4,6-Tribromophenol
- Suggests
  - Phenolic aromatic compound

# V. Tests for Aldehydes and Ketones

## A. 2,4-DNP Test

- Reagent
  - 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine
- Tests
  - Aldehydes
  - Ketones
- Positive observation
  - Yellow/orange precipitate
- Product
  - 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazone

## B. Tollens' Test

- Reagent
  - Ammoniacal silver(I) complex
- Positive observation
  - Silver mirror
- Strong indication
  - Aldehyde
- Aldehyde oxidation
  - Aldehyde → carboxylate

## C. Fehling's Test

- Reagents
  - Fehling A
  - Fehling B
- Positive observation
  - Brick-red Cu₂O precipitate
- Commonly positive
  - Aliphatic aldehydes
  - Reducing sugars

## D. Aldehyde vs Ketone

- 2,4-DNP positive
  - Carbonyl compound
- Tollens positive
  - Aldehyde or reducing substance
- Tollens negative
  - Ketone becomes more likely
- Interpret multiple tests together

# VII. Tests for Amines

## A. Solubility in Dilute HCl

- Formation of ammonium salt
- R-NH₂ + HCl
  - → R-NH₃⁺Cl⁻
- Increased water solubility

## B. Carbylamine Test

- Applies to
  - Primary amines
- Reagents
  - CHCl₃
  - Alcoholic KOH
  - Heat
- Positive observation
  - Characteristic unpleasant isocyanide odour
- Reaction
  - R-NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH
  - → R-NC + 3KCl + 3H₂O

## C. Hinsberg Test

- Reagent
  - Benzenesulfonyl chloride
  - Aqueous alkali

### Primary amine

- Forms sulfonamide
- Sulfonamide dissolves in alkali

### Secondary amine

- Forms sulfonamide
- Generally insoluble in alkali

### Tertiary amine

- Does not form sulfonamide under normal conditions
- Forms salt with acid

# VIII. Amino Acids

## A. General Structure

- General structure
  - H₂N-CHR-COOH
- α-Amino acids
  - NH₂ and COOH attached to same α-carbon
- Important groups
  - Amino group
  - Carboxyl group
  - Side chain R

## B. Zwitterionic Nature

- In solid state and near neutral conditions
  - ⁺NH₃-CHR-COO⁻
- Internal proton transfer
  - -NH₂ accepts H⁺
  - -COOH donates H⁺
- Properties
  - High melting point
  - Ionic character
  - Water solubility varies
- Amphoteric behaviour
  - React with acids
  - React with bases

## C. Classification by Side Chain

### Non-polar

- Glycine
- Alanine
- Valine
- Leucine
- Isoleucine

### Polar uncharged

- Serine
- Threonine
- Cysteine
- Asparagine
- Glutamine

### Acidic

- Aspartic acid
- Glutamic acid

### Basic

- Lysine
- Arginine
- Histidine

### Aromatic

- Phenylalanine
- Tyrosine
- Tryptophan

## D. Amino Acid Identification

### Ninhydrin Test

- General test for
  - α-amino acids
  - Free amino groups
- Reagent
  - Ninhydrin
- Heating
- Positive observation
  - Blue/violet colour
  - Ruhemann's purple
- Important exception
  - Proline and hydroxyproline
    - Secondary amino group
    - Yellow colour rather than typical purple
- Inference
  - Free amino group likely present

### Xanthoproteic Test

- Detects
  - Aromatic amino acids
- Reagent
  - Concentrated HNO₃
- Heating
- Positive observation
  - Yellow colour
- Addition of alkali
  - Colour becomes orange
- Positive amino acids
  - Tyrosine
  - Tryptophan
  - Phenylalanine gives weaker/less characteristic response
- Principle
  - Nitration of aromatic ring

### Identification Logic

- Unknown amino acid
  - Ninhydrin positive
    - Amino acid/free amino group likely
  - Xanthoproteic positive
    - Aromatic amino acid likely
  - Ninhydrin positive + Xanthoproteic positive
    - Consider aromatic amino acids
      - Tyrosine
      - Tryptophan
      - Phenylalanine
  - Ninhydrin positive + Xanthoproteic negative
    - Non-aromatic amino acid becomes more likely

## E. Amino Acid Behaviour with Acids and Bases

### With acid

- Basic amino acid groups become protonated
- NH₂ → NH₃⁺

### With base

- Carboxyl group becomes deprotonated
- COOH → COO⁻

### At isoelectric point

- Net charge
  - Approximately zero
- Amino acid exists mainly as zwitterion
- Solubility may be minimum

# IX. Tests for Proteins

## A. Biuret Test

- Purpose
  - Detect peptide bonds
- Reagents
  - CuSO₄
  - NaOH
- Alkaline medium required
- Positive observation
  - Violet/purple colour
- Principle
  - Cu²⁺ forms a coloured coordination complex with peptide bonds
- Indicates
  - Proteins
  - Peptides with sufficient peptide bonds
- Important
  - Free amino acids generally do not give the Biuret test
  - Because peptide bonds are absent

## B. Ninhydrin Test for Proteins/Amino Acids

- Detects
  - Free amino groups
- Strong response
  - Free amino acids
- Protein response
  - Depends on accessible free amino groups
- Positive colour
  - Blue/violet
- Important distinction
  - Ninhydrin → free amino groups
  - Biuret → peptide bonds

## C. Xanthoproteic Test for Proteins

- Detects
  - Aromatic amino acid residues
- Reagent
  - Concentrated HNO₃
- Positive observation
  - Yellow
  - Orange after alkali
- Indicates
  - Aromatic residues
- Common contributors
  - Tyrosine
  - Tryptophan
  - Phenylalanine weaker

# X. Biuret vs Ninhydrin vs Xanthoproteic

## Biuret Test

- Target
  - Peptide bonds
- Sample
  - Proteins / peptides
- Reagent
  - CuSO₄ + NaOH
- Positive colour
  - Violet/purple
- Does not primarily detect
  - Individual amino acids

## Ninhydrin Test

- Target
  - Free amino groups
- Sample
  - Amino acids
  - Compounds with free amino groups
- Reagent
  - Ninhydrin
- Positive colour
  - Blue/violet
- Exception
  - Proline → yellow

## Xanthoproteic Test

- Target
  - Aromatic amino acid residues
- Sample
  - Certain amino acids
  - Proteins containing aromatic residues
- Reagent
  - Concentrated HNO₃
- Positive colour
  - Yellow → orange with alkali

# XII. Tests for Carbohydrates

## A. Molisch Test

- General carbohydrate test
- Reagents
  - α-Naphthol
  - Concentrated H₂SO₄
- Positive observation
  - Violet/purple ring
- Indicates
  - Carbohydrate

## B. Benedict's Test

- Detects
  - Reducing sugars
- Reagent
  - Benedict's reagent
- Heating
- Observation
  - Green
  - Yellow
  - Orange
  - Red precipitate
- Cu²⁺ → Cu₂O

## C. Fehling's Test

- Detects
  - Reducing sugars
- Positive observation
  - Brick-red Cu₂O precipitate

## D. Barfoed's Test

- Distinguishes
  - Monosaccharides
  - Disaccharides
- Rapid red Cu₂O formation
  - Monosaccharide more likely
- Timing is important

## E. Seliwanoff's Test

- Distinguishes
  - Ketoses
  - Aldoses
- Reagents
  - Resorcinol
  - Concentrated HCl
- Ketose
  - Rapid cherry-red colour
- Aldose
  - Slower/weaker response

## F. Iodine Test

- Detects
  - Starch
- Positive observation
  - Blue/blue-black
- Glycogen
  - Reddish-brown

## G. Osazone Test

- Reagent
  - Phenylhydrazine
- Forms
  - Osazone crystals
- Historically useful
  - Characterisation of reducing sugars
- Limitation
  - Different sugars can produce identical osazones

# XIII. Unknown Organic Compound Identification

## Step 1 — Physical Examination

- State
- Colour
- Melting point
- Boiling point
- Odour
  - Do not directly smell unknown substances

## Step 2 — Solubility Classification

### Water soluble

- Consider
  - Small polar compounds
  - Amino acids
  - Carbohydrates

### Soluble in dilute HCl

- Possible
  - Amine
  - Basic compound

### Soluble in dilute NaOH

- Possible
  - Carboxylic acid
  - Phenol

### Soluble in NaHCO₃

- Strong indication
  - Carboxylic acid

### Insoluble in all

- Consider
  - Hydrocarbon
  - Ester
  - Ether
  - Aldehyde
  - Ketone
  - Higher alcohol

## Step 3 — Functional Group Tests

### Alcohol

- Lucas
- Oxidation
- Sodium metal

### Phenol

- FeCl₃
- Bromine water

### Aldehyde/Ketone

- 2,4-DNP

### Aldehyde

- Tollens
- Fehling

### Carboxylic acid

- NaHCO₃

### Amine

- Carbylamine
- Hinsberg

### Amino acid

- Ninhydrin
- Xanthoproteic

### Protein

- Biuret
- Ninhydrin
- Xanthoproteic

### Carbohydrate

- Molisch
- Benedict
- Fehling
- Barfoed
- Seliwanoff
- Iodine

## Step 4 — Confirmatory Testing

- Never identify solely from one test
- Combine

# XIV. High-Yield Observation → Inference

## Brisk effervescence with NaHCO₃

- CO₂
- Carboxylic acid likely

## Silver mirror

- Tollens positive
- Aldehyde/reducing substance

## Brick-red precipitate

- Fehling/Benedict
- Reducing sugar or suitable reducing compound

## Yellow/orange precipitate

- 2,4-DNP
- Carbonyl compound

## Violet FeCl₃ colour

- Phenolic/enol-type complex possible

## Blue-black colour

- Iodine
- Starch

## Cherry-red colour

- Seliwanoff
- Ketose likely

## Violet ring

- Molisch
- Carbohydrate

## Violet/purple with Biuret

- Peptide bonds
- Protein/peptide

## Blue/violet with Ninhydrin

- Free amino group
- Amino acid likely

## Yellow → orange with Xanthoproteic

- Aromatic amino acid/residue

## Yellow with Ninhydrin

- Proline/hydroxyproline possible

## Turbidity in Lucas test

- Alcohol
- Rate helps classify 1°, 2°, 3°

## Isocyanide odour

- Carbylamine
- Primary amine

# XV. Critical Comparison

## Amino Acid

- Building block of proteins
- Contains
  - Amino group
  - Carboxyl group
- Ninhydrin
  - Positive
- Biuret
  - Negative for free amino acids
- Xanthoproteic
  - Positive only when aromatic group responds

## Peptide

- Contains peptide bonds
- Biuret
  - Can be positive if sufficient peptide bonds
- Ninhydrin
  - Free terminal amino group may respond
- Xanthoproteic
  - Depends on aromatic residues

## Protein

- Contains many peptide bonds
- Biuret
  - Positive
- Ninhydrin
  - Free amino groups can respond
- Xanthoproteic
  - Positive if aromatic amino acid residues are present

# Inorganic Salt Analysis — Anions
## JEE Advanced Practical Chemistry

## I. Qualitative Analysis Strategy

### Objective

- Identify the anion present in an unknown inorganic salt
- Use
  - Preliminary tests
  - Group tests
  - Confirmatory tests
  - Observation → inference
  - Elimination of interfering ions

### Core Principle

- Do NOT identify an anion from a single observation
- Follow
  - Preliminary test
  - Specific reagent
  - Characteristic observation
  - Confirmatory test
  - Final inference

### Important Anions

- CO₃²⁻
- HCO₃⁻
- S²⁻
- SO₃²⁻
- S₂O₃²⁻
- SO₄²⁻
- NO₃⁻
- NO₂⁻
- Cl⁻
- Br⁻
- I⁻
- PO₄³⁻
- C₂O₄²⁻
- CH₃COO⁻
- BO₃³⁻ / borate
- F⁻
- CrO₄²⁻
- MnO₄⁻

# II. Preliminary Examination

## A. Physical Appearance

### Observe

- Colour of salt
- Physical state
- Crystal form
- Odour
- Solubility in water

### Important coloured salts

- Chromate
  - Yellow
- Dichromate
  - Orange
- Permanganate
  - Purple
- Many common anion salts
  - White/colourless

### Odour

- Do NOT directly smell unknown salts
- Perform controlled test if required

# IV. Carbonate CO₃²⁻

## Preliminary Test

### Add dilute HCl

- Brisk effervescence
- Gas evolved
  - CO₂

### Reaction

- CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺
- → CO₂↑ + H₂O

## Confirmatory Test

### Pass gas through limewater

- Limewater turns milky

### Reaction

- CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂
- → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O

### Excess CO₂

- Milkiness disappears

### Reaction

- CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O
- → Ca(HCO₃)₂

## Distinguishing Carbonate from Bicarbonate

### Carbonate

- Dilute acid
  - Brisk effervescence
- Salt solution can be alkaline

### Bicarbonate

- Dilute acid
  - CO₂ evolved
- Phenolphthalein behaviour can help distinguish carbonate/bicarbonate

---

# V. Bicarbonate HCO₃⁻

## Dilute Acid Test

- Effervescence
- CO₂ evolved

### Reaction

- HCO₃⁻ + H⁺
- → CO₂↑ + H₂O

## Confirmation

- CO₂ + limewater
- Limewater becomes milky

## Important

- Carbonate and bicarbonate both produce CO₂
- Additional tests required for differentiation

---

# VI. Sulfide S²⁻

## Dilute Acid Test

### Add dilute HCl

- Gas evolved
- H₂S

### Reaction

- S²⁻ + 2H⁺
- → H₂S↑

## Confirmation

### Lead acetate paper

- Black colour

### Reaction

- H₂S + Pb²⁺
- → PbS↓ + 2H⁺

## Sodium Nitroprusside Test

- Fresh alkaline solution
- Violet/purple colour
- Suggests sulfide

## Important

- H₂S is toxic
- Conduct test under appropriate ventilation

---

# VII. Sulfite SO₃²⁻

## Dilute Acid Test

- Gas evolved
- SO₂

### Reaction

- SO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺
- → SO₂↑ + H₂O

## Confirmation

### Acidified KMnO₄

- Purple colour disappears

### Principle

- SO₂ acts as reducing agent

## Dichromate Test

- Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇
- Orange → green

### Principle

- Cr(VI) reduced to Cr(III)

## Distinguish SO₃²⁻ from CO₃²⁻

### Carbon dioxide

- Limewater milky

### Sulfur dioxide

- Acidified KMnO₄ decolourised

---

# VIII. Thiosulfate S₂O₃²⁻

## Dilute Acid Test

### Add dilute HCl

- SO₂ evolved
- Sulfur precipitated

### Reaction

- S₂O₃²⁻ + 2H⁺
- → SO₂↑ + S↓ + H₂O

## Observation

- Effervescence
- Yellow turbidity/precipitated sulfur

## Distinction from Sulfite

### Sulfite

- SO₂ evolved
- No sulfur precipitate

### Thiosulfate

- SO₂ evolved
- Sulfur precipitate forms

## Iodine Test

- Thiosulfate reduces iodine
- Iodine colour disappears

### Reaction

- 2S₂O₃²⁻ + I₂
- → S₄O₆²⁻ + 2I⁻

---

# IX. Sulfate SO₄²⁻

## Important

- Sulfate generally does NOT evolve gas with dilute HCl

## Confirmatory Test

### Acidified BaCl₂

- Add dilute HCl
- Add BaCl₂

### Observation

- White precipitate

### Reaction

- Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻
- → BaSO₄↓

## Confirmation

### BaSO₄

- White
- Insoluble in dilute mineral acids

## Important Interference

### Carbonate

- Can form BaCO₃
- Dissolves in acid

### Sulfite

- Can form barium sulfite
- Acid treatment can cause decomposition

### Therefore

- Acidify before BaCl₂ test
- Then observe persistent BaSO₄ precipitate

---

# X. Nitrate NO₃⁻

## Preliminary

- No characteristic gas with dilute HCl

## Brown Ring Test

### Reagents

- Fresh FeSO₄ solution
- Concentrated H₂SO₄

### Procedure

- Add FeSO₄ solution to nitrate solution
- Carefully add concentrated H₂SO₄ along the side of test tube
- Formation of layers

### Positive observation

- Brown ring at interface

### Principle

- NO₃⁻ reduced to NO
- NO forms complex with Fe²⁺

### Complex

- [Fe(H₂O)₅(NO)]²⁺
- Brown nitrosyl complex

## Important

- Fresh FeSO₄ required
- Correct layering is important
- Avoid disturbing the interface

---

# XI. Nitrite NO₂⁻

## Griess-Type Reaction

- Nitrite produces nitrous acid under acidic conditions
- HNO₂ participates in diazotisation/coupling reactions
- Characteristic coloured product can form

## KI-Starch Test

- Acidified nitrite can oxidise iodide
- I₂ produced
- Starch
  - Blue colour

### Principle

- NO₂⁻ acts as oxidising agent under suitable conditions

## Important

- Nitrite can interfere with nitrate tests
- Nitrite may need to be removed/considered before nitrate confirmation

---

# XII. Chloride Cl⁻

## Silver Nitrate Test

### Reagent

- AgNO₃
- Acidify appropriately with dilute HNO₃

### Observation

- White precipitate

### Reaction

- Ag⁺ + Cl⁻
- → AgCl↓

## Solubility in NH₃

### Dilute NH₃

- AgCl dissolves

### Reaction

- AgCl + 2NH₃
- ⇌ [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + Cl⁻

## Key Identification

- White AgCl
- Soluble in dilute NH₃

---

# XIII. Bromide Br⁻

## Silver Nitrate Test

- AgNO₃ after suitable acidification
- Cream/pale yellow precipitate

### Reaction

- Ag⁺ + Br⁻
- → AgBr↓

## Ammonia Test

- AgBr
  - Sparingly soluble in dilute NH₃
  - More soluble in concentrated NH₃

## Oxidation Test

### Chlorine water

- Br⁻ oxidised to Br₂

### Observation

- Orange/brown colour

### Principle

- Cl₂ + 2Br⁻
- → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂

## Organic solvent extraction

- Bromine dissolves in organic solvent
- Orange/brown colour

---

# XIV. Iodide I⁻

## Silver Nitrate Test

- Yellow precipitate

### Reaction

- Ag⁺ + I⁻
- → AgI↓

## Ammonia Test

- AgI
  - Insoluble in dilute NH₃
  - Insoluble in concentrated NH₃

## Chlorine Water Test

- I⁻ oxidised to I₂

### Reaction

- Cl₂ + 2I⁻
- → 2Cl⁻ + I₂

## Observation

- Brown solution
- Organic solvent layer
  - Violet/purple iodine colour

## Halide Identification

### Cl⁻

- AgCl
  - White
  - Dissolves in dilute NH₃

### Br⁻

- AgBr
  - Cream
  - Dissolves in concentrated NH₃

### I⁻

- AgI
  - Yellow
  - Insoluble in NH₃

---

# XV. Phosphate PO₄³⁻

## Ammonium Molybdate Test

### Reagents

- Ammonium molybdate
- Concentrated HNO₃
- Warm conditions

### Positive observation

- Yellow precipitate

### Indicates

- Phosphate

## Alternative Test

### Magnesia mixture

- Mg²⁺
- NH₄⁺
- NH₃

### Observation

- White crystalline precipitate

### Product

- MgNH₄PO₄

## Important

- Acid-base conditions are important
- Excessively acidic/basic conditions can affect precipitation

---

# XVI. Oxalate C₂O₄²⁻

## Calcium Chloride Test

- Add CaCl₂

### Observation

- White precipitate

### Reaction

- Ca²⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻
- → CaC₂O₄↓

## Acidified KMnO₄ Test

- Warm acidified oxalate solution
- KMnO₄ decolourises

### Principle

- Oxalate acts as reducing agent

### Reaction concept

- C₂O₄²⁻ → CO₂
- MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺

## Important

- Warming is generally required
- Reaction is slow at room temperature

---

# XVII. Acetate CH₃COO⁻

## Dilute H₂SO₄ Test

- Add dilute acid
- Acetic acid may be liberated

### Possible observation

- Characteristic vinegar-like odour

## Ester Test

### Reagents

- Ethanol
- Concentrated H₂SO₄
- Heat carefully

### Observation

- Fruity ester odour

### Reaction

- CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH
- ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

## Ferric Chloride Test

- Neutral FeCl₃
- Red/brown coloration may occur
- Interpretation requires appropriate conditions

---

# XVIII. Borate BO₃³⁻ / Borate Species

## Turmeric Paper Test

- Acidify borate solution
- Turmeric paper
- Characteristic red/brown colour may develop

## Methanol Flame Test

- Borate + methanol
- In presence of concentrated H₂SO₄
- Volatile borate ester forms

### Observation

- Green flame

## Important

- Flame colour must be interpreted carefully
- Contamination can affect flame tests

---

# XIX. Fluoride F⁻

## Calcium Test

- Ca²⁺ reagent
- Possible CaF₂ precipitation

### Reaction

- Ca²⁺ + 2F⁻
- → CaF₂↓

## Special Test

- Fluoride can attack glass under strongly acidic conditions
- Silicon tetrafluoride / fluorosilicate chemistry may be involved

## Important Safety

- HF-related chemistry is highly hazardous
- Do NOT perform concentrated HF/glass-etching procedures as a routine student test

---

# XX. Chromate CrO₄²⁻

## Physical Observation

- Solution
  - Yellow

## Lead Acetate Test

- Pb²⁺ + CrO₄²⁻
- → PbCrO₄↓

### Observation

- Yellow precipitate

## Silver Nitrate Test

- Ag₂CrO₄ may form
- Brick-red/reddish-brown precipitate

## Acid-Base Equilibrium

### Acidification

- Chromate ⇌ Dichromate

### Equation

- 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺
- ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O

### Colour change

- Chromate
  - Yellow
- Dichromate
  - Orange

---

# XXI. Permanganate MnO₄⁻

## Physical Observation

- Intense purple solution

## Redox Behaviour

- Strong oxidising agent
- Reduction product depends on medium

### Acidic medium

- MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺
- Purple colour disappears

### Neutral/basic conditions

- MnO₂ may form
- Brown/black precipitate

### Strongly alkaline medium

- Manganate formation possible
- Green colour

## Important

- Permanganate is itself strongly coloured
- This can mask observations in other tests

# XXIX. JEE Advanced Trap Concepts

## Trap 1

### "White precipitate with BaCl₂ = sulfate"

- Not necessarily
- Carbonate/sulfite can also form barium salts
- Acidification is important

## Trap 2

### "Gas with dilute HCl = carbonate"

- Not necessarily
- Sulfide
- Sulfite
- Thiosulfate
- Nitrite
- can also produce gases

## Trap 3

### "Yellow AgNO₃ precipitate = iodide"

- Usually AgI
- But observation must be combined with NH₃ solubility and other tests

## Trap 4

### "Tollens-like reducing behaviour"

- Redox tests may have multiple positive species
- Do not equate one positive test with one unique ion

## Trap 5

### "Colour alone identifies anion"

- Chromate and permanganate can be strongly coloured
- But coloured mixtures can mask observations
- Confirm chemically

## Trap 6

### "Acidify with HCl before AgNO₃"

- HCl introduces Cl⁻
- This can produce AgCl
- Therefore halide precipitation tests use appropriate acidification, typically dilute HNO₃ rather than HCl

## Trap 7

### "All precipitates are insoluble"

- Check
  - Solubility in acids
  - Solubility in NH₃
  - Complex formation
  - Redox behaviour
